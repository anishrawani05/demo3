📊 What is Percentage?

A percentage is a number or ratio expressed as a fraction of 100. The term "percent" comes from the Latin "per centum", which means "by the hundred." Percentages are denoted using the percent sign (%).

In simple terms, a percentage tells you how many parts out of one hundred something represents. For example, if you score 85% on a test, it means you answered 85 out of every 100 questions correctly.

Percentages are a universal way of comparing numbers relative to a common scale (100). This makes them incredibly useful for comparing data across different contexts — such as comparing the performance of two students who took tests with different total marks, or comparing the discount on two products with different original prices.

The mathematical relationship can be expressed as:

Percentage = ( Part ÷ Whole ) × 100

Where Part is the portion you're interested in, and Whole is the total or complete quantity.

🧮 How to Calculate Percentage

Calculating a percentage manually involves a few simple steps. Here's the standard method:

  1. Identify the part and the whole: Determine what number represents the "part" and what represents the "whole" (total).
  2. Divide the part by the whole: Use the formula: Part ÷ Whole.
  3. Multiply by 100: Take the result from step 2 and multiply it by 100 to get the percentage.

Example: If you scored 45 marks out of a total of 60 marks:

  • Step 1: Part = 45, Whole = 60
  • Step 2: 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75
  • Step 3: 0.75 × 100 = 75%

This method works for any situation where you want to express a portion as a percentage of the total.

📈 Percentage Increase Formula

Percentage increase is used when a value grows or rises over time. The formula is:

Percentage Increase = ( ( New Value − Old Value ) ÷ Old Value ) × 100

The result is positive when the new value is greater than the old value. A percentage increase of 25% means the value has grown by one-quarter of its original size.

Example: A product price increases from ₹200 to ₹250.

  • Old Value = 200, New Value = 250
  • Change = 250 − 200 = 50
  • Percentage Increase = (50 ÷ 200) × 100 = 25%

📉 Percentage Decrease Formula

Percentage decrease is used when a value declines or shrinks. The formula is:

Percentage Decrease = ( ( Old Value − New Value ) ÷ Old Value ) × 100

The result is positive when the new value is less than the old value. A percentage decrease of 20% means the value has fallen by one-fifth of its original size.

Example: A product price drops from ₹250 to ₹200.

  • Old Value = 250, New Value = 200
  • Change = 250 − 200 = 50
  • Percentage Decrease = (50 ÷ 250) × 100 = 20%

📊 Percentage Difference Formula

Percentage difference is used to compare two numbers without designating one as "old" or "new." It's often used when comparing two measurements, prices, or scores.

Percentage Difference = ( | Value₁ − Value₂ | ÷ ( ( Value₁ + Value₂ ) ÷ 2 ) ) × 100

The absolute value (| |) ensures the result is always positive, representing the relative difference between the two numbers.

Example: Compare 150 and 200.

  • Value₁ = 150, Value₂ = 200
  • Difference = |150 − 200| = 50
  • Average = (150 + 200) ÷ 2 = 175
  • Percentage Difference = (50 ÷ 175) × 100 = 28.57%

🔄 Percentage Change Explained

Percentage change is a measure that quantifies the relative change in a value over time. It is widely used in finance, economics, business, and everyday life to understand growth or decline.

The key distinction between percentage increase and percentage decrease is simply the direction of the change:

  • Positive percentage change = Increase (value has gone up).
  • Negative percentage change = Decrease (value has gone down).

Our calculator handles both scenarios automatically. If the new value is greater than the old value, it shows "Increase" with the percentage. If the new value is smaller, it shows "Decrease".

This concept is essential for understanding trends — whether you're tracking stock prices, sales figures, population growth, or any other metric that changes over time.

🔢 Percentage of a Number Explained

The phrase "percentage of a number" refers to calculating a specific percentage of a given quantity. It's one of the most common percentage calculations in everyday life.

The calculation involves multiplying the number by the percentage expressed as a decimal (percentage ÷ 100).

Example 1: What is 15% of 120?

  • Step 1: 15% = 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15
  • Step 2: 0.15 × 120 = 18

Answer: 15% of 120 is 18.

Example 2: What is 8.5% of 2,000?

  • Step 1: 8.5% = 8.5 ÷ 100 = 0.085
  • Step 2: 0.085 × 2000 = 170

Answer: 8.5% of 2,000 is 170.

This calculation is used everywhere — from calculating tips and discounts to determining tax amounts and interest.

🔍 Finding the Original Value from a Percentage

Sometimes you know a percentage and the resulting part, but you need to find the original (whole) value. This is known as reverse percentage calculation.

Original Value = Part ÷ ( Percentage ÷ 100 )

Example: If 40% of a number is 80, what is the original number?

  • Part = 80, Percentage = 40%
  • Original = 80 ÷ (40 ÷ 100) = 80 ÷ 0.40 = 200

Answer: The original number is 200.

This is particularly useful when you know the discounted price and the discount percentage, and you want to find the original price before the discount.

🎓 Percentage in Exams and Marks

In education, percentages are the most common way to represent a student's performance. Whether it's a school exam, a competitive test, or a university course, percentages provide a standardized metric to compare results.

To calculate the percentage of marks obtained in an exam:

Percentage = ( Marks Obtained ÷ Total Marks ) × 100

For example, if a student scores 320 marks out of a total of 400, the percentage would be:

  • (320 ÷ 400) × 100 = 80%

Many educational institutions also use CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) systems, where percentages are converted to grade points using specific conversion formulas.

For competitive exams like JEE, NEET, CAT, and UPSC, percentages are often used to determine eligibility and rank. A difference of just a few percentage points can significantly impact admissions and career opportunities.

🏢 Percentage in Business and Finance

In the business world, percentages are the language of performance, growth, and profitability. Here's how they're used:

  • Profit Margin: (Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100 — indicates how much profit a company makes for every rupee of revenue.
  • Revenue Growth: ((Current Revenue − Previous Revenue) ÷ Previous Revenue) × 100 — tracks business growth.
  • Market Share: (Company Sales ÷ Total Market Sales) × 100 — shows a company's portion of the total market.
  • Employee Productivity: Percentage of targets achieved or tasks completed.
  • Return on Investment (ROI): ((Gain from Investment − Cost of Investment) ÷ Cost of Investment) × 100.
  • Budget Allocation: Percentage of total budget allocated to different departments or projects.

Investors and analysts rely heavily on percentage-based metrics to evaluate companies, make investment decisions, and forecast future performance.

🛍️ Percentage in Shopping and Discounts

Discounts are one of the most common real-life applications of percentages. Understanding discounts helps you make smarter purchasing decisions and save money.

To calculate the discount amount:

Discount Amount = Original Price × ( Discount Percentage ÷ 100 )

Example: A laptop costs ₹50,000 and is offered at a 20% discount.

  • Discount Amount = 50,000 × 0.20 = ₹10,000
  • Final Price = 50,000 − 10,000 = ₹40,000

Additional discount scenarios include:

  • Successive Discounts: When two or more discounts are applied one after the other (e.g., 10% off, then 5% off).
  • Cashback Offers: A percentage of the purchase amount returned as cashback.
  • Buy One Get One (BOGO): Often equivalent to a 50% discount if both items have the same price.

🧾 Percentage in GST and Tax Calculations

Taxes are almost always calculated as a percentage of the transaction value. In India, the Goods and Services Tax (GST) is applied at different percentage rates depending on the product or service category.

Tax Amount = Transaction Value × ( Tax Rate ÷ 100 )

Common GST rates in India include 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%. For example, if you purchase a product worth ₹10,000 with an 18% GST:

  • Tax Amount = 10,000 × 0.18 = ₹1,800
  • Total Price = 10,000 + 1,800 = ₹11,800

Income tax is also calculated using percentage-based slabs, where different portions of your income are taxed at different rates. Our Income Tax Calculator (available on 99toolhub) uses these percentage-based slabs to compute your tax liability.

📈 Percentage in Profit and Loss

In business, profit and loss are commonly expressed as percentages to evaluate performance and compare different ventures.

Profit Percentage = ( Profit ÷ Cost Price ) × 100 Loss Percentage = ( Loss ÷ Cost Price ) × 100

Example: An item is purchased for ₹500 and sold for ₹650.

  • Profit = 650 − 500 = ₹150
  • Profit Percentage = (150 ÷ 500) × 100 = 30%

Profit and loss percentages are essential for understanding business health, pricing strategies, and investment decisions.

🏦 Percentage in Banking and Interest

Banking and finance are built on percentages. Interest rates, which are always expressed as percentages, determine how much you earn on savings or pay on loans.

  • Simple Interest: Interest calculated on the principal amount only.
  • Compound Interest: Interest calculated on both the principal and accumulated interest.
  • Annual Percentage Rate (APR): The annual rate charged for borrowing or earned through investment.

For example, if you deposit ₹10,000 in a savings account with an annual interest rate of 5%:

  • Interest earned in one year = 10,000 × 0.05 = ₹500

Credit cards, home loans, car loans, and personal loans all use percentage-based interest rates to determine your monthly payments and total repayment amounts.

⚠️ Common Percentage Calculation Mistakes

Even though percentages are simple in concept, mistakes are surprisingly common. Here are the most frequent errors to avoid:

  • Confusing Percentage and Percentage Points: A change from 10% to 15% is a 5 percentage point increase, but a 50% relative increase.
  • Using the Wrong Base Value: Always use the original value as the base for percentage change calculations.
  • Forgetting to Multiply by 100: After dividing part by whole, don't forget to multiply by 100 to get the percentage.
  • Misplacing the Decimal Point: 25% = 0.25, not 2.5. A small decimal error can lead to huge inaccuracies.
  • Adding or Subtracting Percentages Directly: If a price increases by 10% and then decreases by 10%, it does not return to the original price.
  • Not Accounting for Successive Discounts Correctly: Two discounts of 10% each are not the same as a single 20% discount.
  • Using Percentage Change for Small Numbers: When values are very small, percentage changes can be misleadingly large.
Pro Tip: Always double-check your base value and the direction of change. When in doubt, use a calculator like the one above to verify your results.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is a percentage?
A percentage is a number or ratio expressed as a fraction of 100. It is denoted by the symbol % and represents "parts per hundred."
How do I calculate a percentage of a number?
Multiply the number by the percentage (as a decimal). For example, 20% of 150 = (20 ÷ 100) × 150 = 30.
What is the formula for percentage increase?
Percentage Increase = ((New Value − Old Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100. This gives the percentage by which the value has grown.
What is the formula for percentage decrease?
Percentage Decrease = ((Old Value − New Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100. This gives the percentage by which the value has declined.
How do I find the original number from a percentage?
If you know the percentage and the part, divide the part by (percentage ÷ 100). For example, if 25% of a number is 50, the original number is 50 ÷ 0.25 = 200.
What is the difference between percentage and percentage points?
A percentage point is the absolute difference between two percentage values. For example, an increase from 20% to 25% is a 5 percentage point increase, but a 25% relative increase.
Can I use this calculator for GST and tax calculations?

How do I calculate a discount percentage?
Discount Percentage = (Discount Amount ÷ Original Price) × 100. For example, if you save ₹200 on a ₹1,000 item, the discount percentage is (200 ÷ 1000) × 100 = 20%.
What is a percentage difference?
Percentage difference measures the relative difference between two numbers, regardless of which is larger. It uses the average of the two numbers as the base.

💡 Tips for Accurate Percentage Calculations

Follow these tips to ensure your percentage calculations are always accurate:

  • Always use the correct base: For percentage change, use the original value as the denominator.
  • Convert percentages to decimals correctly: 25% = 0.25, not 2.5.
  • Double-check your inputs: A small typo can completely change the result.
  • Use parentheses when calculating: (Part ÷ Whole) × 100, not Part ÷ Whole × 100 (which would be interpreted differently in some calculators).
  • Understand the context: Be clear about whether you're calculating a percentage of a number, a percentage change, or a percentage difference.
  • Round appropriately: For financial calculations, round to two decimal places. For general purposes, one or two decimal places is usually sufficient.
  • Verify with a second method: If possible, cross-check your results using a different formula or tool.
