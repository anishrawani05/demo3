📖 What is GST? Basics

GST stands for Goods and Services Tax. It is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based tax that is levied on every value addition. GST was introduced in India on 1 July 2017 and replaced a complex web of cascading indirect taxes such as VAT, service tax, excise duty, and more. The idea behind GST was to create a unified tax system that would make India a single common market and boost economic growth.

GST is a consumption-based tax, meaning it is levied at the point of consumption rather than the point of origin. This ensures that the tax revenue goes to the state where the goods or services are consumed, not where they are produced. GST applies to the supply of goods and services, and it is collected at every stage of the supply chain — from manufacturing to the final sale to the consumer.

📌 Key Features of GST

  • One Nation, One Tax: GST subsumed multiple central and state taxes into a single tax system, simplifying compliance and reducing the cascading effect of tax-on-tax.
  • Dual GST Model: India follows a dual GST model where both the Centre and States levy GST simultaneously. CGST (Central GST) is collected by the Centre, and SGST (State GST) is collected by the states on intra-state transactions. For inter-state transactions, IGST (Integrated GST) is levied and collected by the Centre, which is then shared with the states.
  • Destination-Based: GST is collected at the point of consumption, ensuring that the state where the goods are consumed gets the tax revenue.
  • Input Tax Credit (ITC): Businesses can claim credit for the GST they have paid on their purchases (inputs). This ensures that tax is levied only on the value added at each stage, eliminating the cascading effect.
  • Digitisation: GST is entirely technology-driven through the GSTN (Goods and Services Tax Network) portal, making compliance easier and more transparent.

🧮 GST Formula Math

The GST calculation is based on simple mathematical formulas. Whether you are adding GST to a price or extracting GST from a total, the formulas are straightforward. Here's a detailed breakdown:

📌 1. Exclusive GST (Adding GST to a Base Price)

When the price you have does not include GST, you need to add the tax to get the final total. The formulas are:

GST Amount = (Original Cost × GST Rate) / 100
Total Price = Original Cost + GST Amount
Example: Original cost ₹1,000, GST 18% → GST = ₹180, Total = ₹1,180

📌 2. Inclusive GST (Extracting GST from a Total Price)

When the price you have already includes GST, you need to work backwards to find the base amount and the tax component. The formulas are:

GST Amount = (Total Price × GST Rate) / (100 + GST Rate)
Original Cost = Total Price – GST Amount
Example: Total price ₹1,180, GST 18% → GST = ₹180, Base = ₹1,000

📊 GST Rates in India 2026

As per the latest GST Council updates for 2026, the following GST rates are applicable. The rates have been streamlined to four main slabs: 0%, 5%, 18%, and 40%. Here is a detailed breakdown:

GST Rate Category Examples
0% Nil / Exempt Fresh fruits & vegetables, milk, eggs, bread, salt, educational services, healthcare services
5% Essential / Mass Consumption Edible oils, sugar, tea, spices, coal, fertilisers, railway tickets (economy), packaged food items
18% Standard Rate Most goods and services: smartphones, laptops, FMCG products, AC restaurants, telecom services, financial services, IT services
40% Luxury / Sin / Demerit Automobiles, motorcycles, aerated beverages, tobacco products, pan masala, luxury watches, high-end cosmetics, cement

📌 Special Notes for 2026

  • The 28% slab has been merged into the 40% slab for luxury and sin goods.
  • The 12% slab has been merged into the 18% slab for standard goods and services.
  • Essential items continue to enjoy 0% and 5% rates to protect the common man.
  • Gold and jewellery continue to attract a special rate of 3% (not included in the main slabs).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions FAQ

What are the GST rates for 2026?
For 2026, the GST rates in India are 0%, 5%, 18%, and 40%. The 12% and 28% slabs have been merged into 18% and 40% respectively, streamlining the tax structure.
What is the difference between exclusive and inclusive GST?
Exclusive GST means the price does not include tax — GST is added on top. Inclusive GST means the price already includes tax — the GST is embedded in the displayed price.

Who needs to register for GST in India?
Any business with an annual turnover exceeding ₹40 lakhs (₹20 lakhs for special category states) must register for GST. For service providers, the threshold is ₹20 lakhs (₹10 lakhs for special category states).
What is Input Tax Credit (ITC) under GST?
ITC allows businesses to claim credit for the GST they have paid on purchases and services used for business purposes. This prevents the cascading effect of tax and ensures that GST is levied only on the value added at each stage.
