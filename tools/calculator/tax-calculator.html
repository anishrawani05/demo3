💰 What is Income Tax?

Income tax is a direct tax levied by the Government of India on the income earned by individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), companies, firms, and other entities. It is governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961, which is the primary legislation that defines the rules, tax rates, exemptions, and procedures for taxation in India.

The government uses income tax revenue to fund public services such as infrastructure development, education, healthcare, defense, and various welfare schemes. As a responsible citizen, paying income tax is not just a legal obligation but also a contribution to the nation's progress.

Income tax is calculated on the total income (also called gross total income) of a person for a financial year, after allowing deductions and exemptions under various sections of the Income-tax Act. The financial year runs from 1st April to 31st March of the following year. For example, the financial year 2026-27 runs from 1st April 2026 to 31st March 2027, and the corresponding assessment year is 2027-28 (the year in which the tax is assessed and paid).

The income tax system in India is progressive, meaning that individuals with higher incomes are taxed at higher rates. This is achieved through a slab system, where different portions of income are taxed at different rates. Our calculator simplifies this process by computing the tax based on the applicable slabs for your specific category and regime choice.

📐 Income Tax Formula

The income tax computation formula used by our calculator follows the structure defined by the Income-tax Act, 1961:

Income Tax Calculation Formula Step 1: Compute tax on total income as per applicable tax slabs Step 2: Apply rebate under section 87A (if eligible) Step 3: Tax after rebate = Tax – Rebate Step 4: Apply surcharge (if total income exceeds ₹50 lakhs) Step 5: Apply health & education cess @ 4% on (Tax after rebate + Surcharge) Total Tax Liability = Tax after rebate + Surcharge + Cess

The tax slabs are applied progressively — each slab's rate is applied only to the income that falls within that slab range, not the entire income. This is called marginal taxation.

📋 How Taxable Income is Calculated

Taxable income (or net taxable income) is the income on which tax is actually computed. It is derived from your gross total income after allowing deductions, exemptions, and allowances as per the Income-tax Act.

Here's the flow of how taxable income is calculated:

  • Gross Total Income: Sum of all income from five heads — Salary, House Property, Business/Profession, Capital Gains, and Other Sources.
  • Deductions under Chapter VI-A: Amounts deducted from gross total income under sections 80C, 80D, 80G, etc.
  • Net Taxable Income: Gross Total Income – Deductions under Chapter VI-A – Other exemptions (e.g., HRA, LTA).

It is this net taxable income that is subject to tax at the applicable slab rates. Our calculator uses this income as the input to compute the tax liability.

📊 Tax Slabs Explained

Tax slabs are the income ranges to which different tax rates apply. India follows a progressive tax system, meaning higher income is taxed at higher rates. The Income-tax Act, 1961, provides two tax regimes — the old regime and the new regime (introduced under section 115BAC).

📌 Old Tax Regime (2026-27)

  • Less than 60 years: Up to ₹2.5L – 0%, ₹2.5L–₹5L – 5%, ₹5L–₹10L – 20%, Above ₹10L – 30%
  • 60 to 80 years (Senior Citizen): Up to ₹3L – 0%, ₹3L–₹5L – 5%, ₹5L–₹10L – 20%, Above ₹10L – 30%
  • Above 80 years (Super Senior): Up to ₹5L – 0%, ₹5L–₹10L – 20%, Above ₹10L – 30%

📌 New Tax Regime (2026-27) – Default Regime

  • All age groups: Up to ₹3L – 0%, ₹3L–₹6L – 5%, ₹6L–₹9L – 10%, ₹9L–₹12L – 15%, ₹12L–₹15L – 20%, Above ₹15L – 30%

The new regime is the default regime from FY 2023-24 onwards. Taxpayers can choose to opt out of the new regime and revert to the old regime by declaring so while filing their ITR.

⚖️ Old Tax Regime vs New Tax Regime

The introduction of the new tax regime under section 115BAC gave taxpayers a choice. Here's a comparison:

  • New Regime: Lower tax rates but fewer deductions and exemptions available.
  • Old Regime: Higher tax rates but allows a wide range of deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, etc.)

Which one is better? It depends on your investment and deduction pattern. Our calculator lets you compare both regimes easily — simply toggle the "Opt out of new regime" option and observe the difference in tax liability.

🧾 Standard Deduction Explained

The standard deduction is a flat deduction from salary income, introduced to reduce the compliance burden on salaried employees. It was reintroduced in Budget 2019, replacing the earlier transport allowance and medical reimbursement.

Amount: ₹50,000 per annum (for FY 2026-27). This deduction is allowed to all salaried individuals, irrespective of their income level. It is directly deducted from the gross salary, reducing the taxable income. For pensioners, the standard deduction is also available.

Our calculator automatically accounts for standard deduction when computing tax on salary income, ensuring accurate results.

🧩 Income Tax Components

Your total tax liability comprises three main components:

  • Income Tax: The base tax computed on your net taxable income as per the applicable slabs.
  • Surcharge: An additional charge levied on the tax amount if your total income exceeds certain thresholds (₹50 lakhs, ₹1 crore, ₹2 crore, ₹5 crore).
  • Health & Education Cess: A 4% cess on the total of income tax and surcharge, used to fund health and education initiatives.

Rebate under section 87A is applied before surcharge and cess, making it a crucial component for taxpayers with income up to ₹5-7 lakhs.

🏥 Health & Education Cess Explained

The Health and Education Cess was introduced in 2018 (replacing the earlier Education Cess and Secondary and Higher Education Cess) to fund health and education initiatives across India.

  • Rate: 4% of the total income tax liability (including surcharge).
  • Applicability: Applies to all taxpayers — individuals, HUFs, firms, and companies.
  • Example: If your income tax (after rebate and surcharge) is ₹1,00,000, the cess would be ₹4,000 (4% of ₹1,00,000).

📈 Surcharge Explained

A surcharge is an additional tax levied on individuals with high incomes. The rates are as follows (for AY 2026-27):

  • Total income > ₹50 lakhs up to ₹1 crore: 10%
  • Total income > ₹1 crore up to ₹2 crores: 15%
  • Total income > ₹2 crores up to ₹5 crores: 25%
  • Total income > ₹5 crores: 37%

The surcharge is calculated on the income tax amount (after rebate, if any). It is not levied on the total income directly, but on the tax payable.

💡 Tax Saving Tips

Here are some practical tips to legally minimize your tax liability:

  • Maximize Section 80C Investments: Invest up to ₹1.5 lakhs in PPF, ELSS, NSC, etc.
  • Claim Section 80D: Deduct up to ₹25,000 for health insurance premiums (₹50,000 for senior citizens).
  • Use Section 80G: Donate to eligible charities and claim deductions.
  • Claim HRA Exemption: If you live in a rented house, claim House Rent Allowance exemption.
  • Invest in NPS: Under section 80CCD(1B), claim an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000.
  • Education Loan Interest: Claim deduction on interest paid on education loans under section 80E.
  • Choose the Right Regime: Compare old and new regimes to see which gives you lower tax.

📋 Eligible Tax Deductions (Chapter VI-A)

Under the old tax regime, you can claim deductions under various sections to reduce your taxable income. Some key sections are:

  • Section 80C: ₹1.5 lakhs — LIC, PPF, ELSS, NSC, Tuition fees, Principal repayment of home loan.
  • Section 80CCD(1): ₹1.5 lakhs (additional ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B) for NPS).
  • Section 80D: ₹25,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens) — Health insurance premiums.
  • Section 80E: Interest on education loans (no limit, deduction for 8 years).
  • Section 80G: Donations to charitable institutions (50% or 100% of donation).
  • Section 80TTA: ₹10,000 — Interest on savings bank account.
  • Section 80TTB: ₹50,000 — Interest on deposits for senior citizens.

Remember that the new tax regime does not allow most of these deductions, which is why comparing both regimes is essential.

🏦 Investment Options for Tax Saving

Here are the most popular tax-saving investment instruments under section 80C:

  • PPF (Public Provident Fund): 15-year lock-in, tax-free interest, safe investment.
  • ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme): 3-year lock-in, equity-based, potential for high returns.
  • NSC (National Savings Certificate): 5-year lock-in, fixed interest rate.
  • Tax-Saving FDs: 5-year lock-in, fixed interest, safe.
  • Life Insurance Premiums: Premiums paid for your own, spouse, or children's policies.
  • Principal Repayment of Home Loan: The principal amount repaid on a home loan is eligible for deduction.
  • Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana: For girl child, 21-year maturity, high interest.

Additionally, NPS (National Pension System) offers an extra ₹50,000 deduction under section 80CCD(1B) beyond the ₹1.5 lakh limit.

🧑‍💼 Salary Income Tax Guide

Salary income is one of the most common sources of income. Here's how salary income is taxed:

  • Salary: Basic pay, allowances (DA, HRA, LTA, etc.), bonuses, and commissions are all taxable.
  • Standard Deduction: ₹50,000 is deducted directly from the gross salary.
  • HRA Exemption: If you live in a rented house, you can claim exemption on HRA.
  • Tax Deducted at Source (TDS): Your employer deducts TDS on your salary as per your estimated tax liability.
  • Form 16: Your employer issues Form 16 annually, which details your salary, deductions, and TDS.

Use our calculator to estimate your tax liability for the year and plan your investments accordingly.

🏢 Business Income Tax Guide

Business income is taxed differently from salary. Here are the key aspects:

  • Presumptive Taxation: Under sections 44AD, 44ADA, and 44AE, small businesses can declare income at a prescribed percentage of turnover (6% for businesses, 50% for professionals).
  • Actual Income: If you don't opt for presumptive taxation, you must maintain books of accounts and declare actual income after deducting expenses.
  • Deductions: All business expenses — rent, salaries, utilities, raw materials, depreciation — are deductible.
  • Advance Tax: Businesses are required to pay advance tax in installments throughout the year.

📈 Capital Gains Tax Overview

Capital gains arise from the sale of capital assets like property, shares, mutual funds, gold, etc. There are two types:

  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): Assets held for less than 36 months (24 months for immovable property, 12 months for listed shares).
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): Assets held for more than the specified period.

Tax Rates:

  • LTCG on listed shares/equity mutual funds: 10% on gains exceeding ₹1 lakh (without indexation).
  • LTCG on other assets: 20% with indexation.
  • STCG on listed shares: 15% (if securities transaction tax is paid).
  • STCG on other assets: As per normal slab rates.

Our calculator focuses on income tax from salary and business income. For capital gains, consult your CA or use a specialized calculator.

📆 Advance Tax Explained

Advance tax is the income tax you pay in installments throughout the year, instead of paying the entire amount at the end of the year. It applies to:

  • Salaried individuals whose TDS is less than ₹10,000 of their total tax liability.
  • Business owners, professionals, freelancers, and others whose tax liability exceeds ₹10,000.

The due dates for advance tax (for individuals) are:

  • 15th June: 15% of total tax liability
  • 15th September: 45%
  • 15th December: 75%
  • 15th March: 100%

Failure to pay advance tax can attract interest under sections 234B and 234C.

🏷️ TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) Explained

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a mechanism where the payer deducts tax at the time of making a payment and deposits it with the government. TDS is applicable on various payments like salary, rent, professional fees, interest, etc.

  • Salary TDS: Your employer deducts TDS based on your estimated income and applicable deductions.
  • Interest TDS: Banks deduct TDS on interest income when it exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens).
  • Rent TDS: TDS on rent payment where annual rent exceeds ₹2.4 lakhs.
  • Form 26AS: The annual tax statement that shows all TDS deducted on your behalf.

TDS is not an additional tax — it is a prepayment of your tax liability. You can claim credit for TDS while filing your ITR.

⚠️ Common Tax Calculation Mistakes

Avoid these common mistakes to ensure accurate tax computation:

  • Choosing the wrong regime: Not comparing old and new regimes before filing.
  • Missing deductions: Forgetting to claim eligible deductions under 80C, 80D, etc.
  • Incorrect income reporting: Not including all sources of income (e.g., interest, capital gains).
  • Not accounting for HRA: Not claiming HRA exemption if living in a rented house.
  • Ignoring advance tax: Not paying advance tax and attracting interest penalties.
  • Incorrect TDS credit: Failing to match TDS with Form 26AS.
  • Not filing ITR on time: Missing the deadline can lead to late fees and interest.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the old and new tax regimes?
The old regime allows various deductions (80C, 80D, etc.) but has higher tax rates. The new regime has lower rates but does not allow most deductions. You can choose whichever regime results in lower tax.
What is the rebate under section 87A?
Section 87A provides a rebate on income tax if your total income is up to ₹5,00,000 (old regime) or ₹7,00,000 (new regime). The rebate amount is limited to ₹12,500.
What is surcharge and who pays it?
Surcharge is an additional tax on individuals earning more than ₹50 lakhs. Rates range from 10% to 37%, depending on the income level.
What is health and education cess?
A 4% cess on the total tax (including surcharge) that funds government health and education schemes. It applies to all taxpayers.
What is the standard deduction for salaried employees?
A flat ₹50,000 deduction from salary income under the old tax regime. In the new regime, standard deduction is ₹50,000 (for FY 2026-27).
What is the maximum deduction under section 80C?
₹1,50,000 per financial year. This limit is combined with section 80CCC and 80CCD(1).
Can I claim both old and new regime deductions?
No, you must choose either the old regime or the new regime. The new regime does not allow most deductions.
What is the assessment year?
The assessment year is the year following the financial year, during which you file your income tax return. For FY 2026-27, the AY is 2027-28.
What is TDS?
TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is a mechanism where the payer deducts tax at the time of making a payment (salary, interest, rent, etc.) and deposits it with the government.
How can I save tax legally?
You can save tax by investing in 80C instruments, taking health insurance (80D), donating to charities (80G), claiming HRA, and choosing the right tax regime.
Do senior citizens get higher exemption limits?
Yes, senior citizens (60–80 years) have a higher basic exemption limit of ₹3,00,000. Super senior citizens (80+ years) have an exemption limit of ₹5,00,000 in the old regime.
What is the difference between HUF and Individual?
An HUF (Hindu Undivided Family) is a separate tax entity that can hold property and income. Taxed similarly to an individual, but with its own PAN and assessment rules.
What is advance tax and who needs to pay it?
Advance tax is paid in installments throughout the year if your tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 (after TDS). It applies to self-employed individuals and businesses.
Can I use this calculator for NRI tax calculation?
Yes, the calculator allows you to select residential status as "Non-Resident (NRI)" and computes tax accordingly, though NRI tax rules may have additional complexities.
What is the deadline for filing ITR?
For individuals and HUFs (non-audit), the due date is 31st July of the assessment year. For businesses requiring audit, it is 31st October.
Is this calculator updated with the latest tax laws?
Yes, our calculator uses the tax rates and rules as per the Finance Act, 2026, applicable for FY 2026-27.
What happens if I don't pay advance tax on time?
You may be liable to pay interest under sections 234B (default in payment of advance tax) and 234C (deferment of advance tax).
Can I claim deduction for home loan interest?
Yes, under section 24, you can claim a deduction of up to ₹2,00,000 per year for interest on home loan (for self-occupied property).
Is the data I enter stored anywhere?
No. All calculations are performed locally in your browser. Your data is never sent to any server, stored, or shared. Your privacy is fully protected.
What is Form 26AS?
Form 26AS is a consolidated annual tax statement that shows the TDS deducted on your behalf, tax paid by you (advance tax, self-assessment tax), and other tax-related details.

📖 Income Tax Glossary

Assessment Year (AY)
The year in which income tax is assessed on the income earned in the previous financial year.
BMR
Basic Exemption Limit — the threshold above which income becomes taxable.
Cess
A tax levied on tax — used for specific purposes like health and education.
Deduction
Amount subtracted from gross total income to arrive at net taxable income (e.g., 80C).
Exemption
Income that is not taxable (e.g., agricultural income, HRA partially).
HUF
Hindu Undivided Family — a tax entity for Hindu families with a common ancestor.
ITR
Income Tax Return — the form filed with the tax authorities declaring income and tax.
PAN
Permanent Account Number — a unique 10-digit alphanumeric identifier issued by the Income Tax Department.
Rebate
A reduction in the tax payable (e.g., section 87A).
Surcharge
An additional tax on high-income individuals above the regular tax.
TDS
Tax Deducted at Source — tax deducted by the payer at the time of payment.
Tax Slab
The income range to which a specific tax rate applies.

📌 Tax Planning Tips

Tax planning is not just about saving tax — it's about optimizing your financial decisions for long-term wealth creation. Here are some strategic tips:

  • Start early: Plan your tax-saving investments at the beginning of the financial year, not in March.
  • Diversify: Don't put all your money into one 80C instrument. Spread across PPF, ELSS, and others for risk management.
  • Use NPS: The additional ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B) is a great way to save tax while building retirement corpus.
  • Health insurance: Get health insurance for yourself and senior citizen parents to claim up to ₹75,000 under 80D.
  • Home loan benefits: Claim both principal (80C) and interest (24) on your home loan.
  • Consider the new regime: If you don't have significant deductions, the new regime may be more beneficial.
  • Donate wisely: Donations to eligible charities under 80G can reduce tax while supporting good causes.
  • Review and adjust: Regularly review your investments and tax plan to align with your income changes.

📋 Important Notes Before Filing ITR

  • Verify Form 26AS: Ensure all TDS and tax payments are reflected in your Form 26AS.
  • Choose correct ITR form: Use the appropriate form — ITR-1 for salaried, ITR-3 for business, etc.

  • Check eligibility for rebate: Ensure you correctly claim rebate under section 87A if eligible.
  • Avoid mistakes: Cross-check all numbers — income, deductions, TDS, and tax paid.
  • File on time: The due date is 31st July for individuals. Late filing can attract interest and penalty.
